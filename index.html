01 / About

Who I am

A short introduction to my background, how I work, and what drives my interest in engineering.

Cape Town, South Africa

I'm a third-year Electrical and Computer Engineering student at the University of Cape Town, working across embedded systems, high-performance computing, circuit design, and applied machine learning. I like problems that sit at the boundary of disciplines — where a firmware decision affects a signal-processing result, or a hardware constraint shapes a software architecture.

My recent work centres on a multi-node ESP32-S3 WiFi sensing mesh that detects motion using channel state information rather than cameras — a project that has taken me from low-level firmware debugging to dashboard design to writing about the ethics of sensing technology.

Outside of coursework, I'm interested in robotics, software-defined radio, and how engineers can build technology responsibly. I care about writing code and reports that other people can actually pick up and understand.

WiFi Motion Detection Mesh

A 3-node ESP32-S3 system that detects motion using WiFi channel state information (CSI) and RSSI, instead of cameras — useful for privacy-preserving occupancy sensing. I built the multi-node firmware, debugged ESP-NOW peer registration and RSSI reporting, and designed the live visualisation dashboard.

Design report (PDF) Demo video
VerilogFPGATeam Project

StarCore-1 — CORDIC Rotation Co-Processor

As part of a group HPES project, I integrated a CORDIC-based Coordinate Rotation Unit into the StarCore-1 processor for satellite attitude determination — writing Verilog source, testbenches, and contributing to the IEEE-style final report and presentation.

Final report GitHub repo

Medical Imaging & Classification

A deep learning pipeline for classifying pneumonia in the PneumoniaMNIST dataset, comparing a custom CNN against a ResNet18 baseline, later extended to a COVID-19 radiography dataset. Also explored MRI image reconstruction, including low-pass filtering in k-space to study the trade-off between scan speed and image detail.

Interactive 5G / SDR Exhibit

Research into software-defined radio hardware (HackRF, USRP, LimeSDR) and open-source 5G tools, exploring how to design a portable, interactive exhibit that makes invisible RF signals visible to a public audience.
